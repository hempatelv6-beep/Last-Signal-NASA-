### Why these rankings

- **Left Behind (#1):** it turns abstract facts into experiences. You *wait* for a radio signal and *watch* a marsquake. It works without internet, offers a motion-reduced mode, and has a wide catalogue. It loses a little on story because it's a tour more than a narrative.
- **EXOARCHIVE (#2):** the best engineering and sourcing. Live NASA data actually works, every value is labelled live or cached, and it's bilingual. It's an excellent *archive*, but the challenge asks for *storytelling*.
- **Perfectreviewer (#3):** the best *story design*. One arc per object, a clear split between fact and theory, and a question handed to the reader at the end. It would rank #1 if the empty chapters and draft facts were fixed.
- **StarBound (#4):** the clearest focus on school-age kids (mascot, voice, badges), but a very large codebase.
- **CUỘI (#5):** the most original concept. A national folk tale makes NASA hardware feel personal to its audience.
- **Lower ranks:** most share the "standard template" (hero → cards → timeline → quiz) with thin narrative, or have accuracy problems (marsdot.uz, SPACE GRAVEYARD) or overload (v2).

## What judges will probably care about

The 2026 Judging Guide is only released on 13 November. Past Space Apps guides scored projects on roughly **Impact, Creativity, Validity (does it work / is it accurate), Relevance (to the challenge) and Presentation**. **[K]: re-check against the 2026 guide on 13 November.** The challenge text itself stresses *school-age audience*, *narrative over lists*, *"show how missions operated"*, the link from *engineering to science*, and *accuracy*.
